# Region A by analyticity in k: a design note (the Bridge's ruling (C); text only, nothing runs)
**Office ansatz-machine, 2026-11-29.** The question: can non-integrability across region A, k ∈ [3, 4] at spin 3/5 (and every other uncovered stretch), follow from the certificates we already have plus analyticity in k, with no new k-uniform integration in A?

**Short answer: yes, for all of A except possibly a FINITE set of k.**
- Region A lies inside ONE loop picture, the circle picture, valid up to k_cross ≥ 4.02 at every spin. So no continuation across the A/B switch is needed.
- What has to be certified is **loop continuity, not monodromy enclosures:** no singular point of the NVE coefficient touches the chosen loops for any real k in a stretch, plus a homotopy check wherever the loops are redrawn. That is the cheap kind of check (sweeps and boxes, as in C2-L), not the kind that failed in A.
- **One rigorous point value per connected stretch** (F ≠ 0 at a single k) then makes each bad condition hold only at isolated k.
- **An explicit bound on how many exceptions there can be** is available from Jensen's formula with a crude Grönwall bound.

Neon's outside view (relayed, UNVERIFIED) proposed this; the checking below is mine.

## 1. The bad set is a set of zeros of real-analytic functions
**Lemma (group theory).** Let G ⊂ SL₂(ℂ) be generated by M0 and M1, and suppose:
- **(i)** tr[M1, M0] ≠ 2 (F ≠ 0);
- **(ii)** tr M0 ≠ 0 and tr M1 ≠ 0;
- **(iii)** tr M1 is not the trace of any element of a finite primitive subgroup of SL₂ (the binary tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral groups). Those traces form a finite set T_fin of algebraic numbers, so this is P_fin(tr M1) ≠ 0, the condition the region counts and v7 already use.

Then the Zariski closure of G is SL₂, so G_v⁰ = SL₂ and the MRS chain applies.

*Proof.* The algebraic subgroups of SL₂ are:
- **reducible:** they fix a line, which forces tr[M1, M0] = 2, excluded by (i);
- **imprimitive:** in the normaliser of a torus, every element is diagonal or has trace 0. By (ii) both M0 and M1 are diagonal in the same basis, so they commute and F = 0, again excluded by (i);
- **finite primitive:** every element has trace in T_fin, excluded by (iii);
- **SL₂ itself.** □

*Note.* "tr M1 ∉ [−2, 2]", used in the certificates, is a sufficient stand-in for (iii) plus tr M1 ≠ 0. It is **not** a discrete condition, and in the analytic argument (iii) must replace it. A real-analytic tr M1 can sit inside [−2, 2] on a whole interval, but it can equal one of finitely many constants only at isolated k, unless it is constant.

**Analyticity.** Fix based loops L0 and L1 such that, for every k in a real interval J, no singular point of the NVE coefficient q(z; k) lies on L0 ∪ L1 ∪ (the normalisation path [b, +∞)). Then q is analytic in (z, k) on a neighbourhood of (those paths) × J, by Lemma K's argument with "|k| < δ" replaced by "k ∈ J":
- c0 and c0′ are integrals from +∞ of c0″ = −√(8B/5)·a³/(z² + a²)³ on the branch fixed at +∞;
- they are holomorphic in k as long as no zero of B meets the path.

Picard iteration then makes M0(k) and M1(k) holomorphic on a complex neighbourhood of J. So F, tr M0, tr M1 and P_fin(tr M1) are real-analytic on J.

**Consequence.** If each of F, tr M0, tr M1 and P_fin(tr M1) is nonzero at ONE point of J, each vanishes only on a discrete subset of J, and so only finitely often on any compact sub-interval.
- A rigorous point evaluation at a single k suffices. Route (i)'s node values are exactly that, and so is any certified segment.
- No k-uniform enclosure is needed in A.

## 2. The Bridge's questions
### Q1. Do the singularities collide, or does the branch structure change, for real k in (0, 30/7)? Can that be certified cheaply?
The singular points of q (MRS §3): ±ia (log branch points of c0), the zeros of B(u², k) (square-root branch points of c0), r± (zeros of P), p± (zeros of f0² + c0²), and z = ∞ (ordinary for E² ≠ μ²).
- **The B zeros collide with ±i at k = 4,** where B = 6(w + 1)². This is the only collision of c0's branch points inside (0, 30/7).
  - Below 4 they are four complex points with |u| < 1. Above 4 they are ±iy1, ±iy2 with 0 < y1 < 1 < y2, and y2 → ∞ as k → 30/7.
  - The k = 4 collision is **inside the circle** |z| = R0 (|u| ≤ 1 < R0/a). Analyticity of a loop's monodromy needs only that nothing touches the LOOP, so a collision strictly inside is harmless (MRS §5e says the same for the circle data).
- **The outer pair ±iy2 crosses the circle at k_cross** (4.02–4.2 by spin; the v7 closure check, numerical). That is the circle picture's real limit, and it lies above 4.
- **The endpoints:**
  - at k → 0 the B zeros pinch the real line at u = ±1/√3. This is far from every loop (≥ 0.744, MRS §3); Lemma K covers it, and F is analytic at k = 0;
  - at k = 30/7 two B zeros escape to ∞ and the traces are not analytic (v7 plan §3e). So 30/7 is a genuine endpoint, covered by the interval sliver.
- **r± and p± (transcendental) move continuously.** They cannot pinch the real outer axes, because f0² + c0² > 0 there (c0 ≠ 0 at the rod ends, MRS §3). What must be certified is only that none of them touches a loop for k ∈ J.
- **Cheap certification, all real-k only:**
  - the B zeros by the quadratic formula over k-intervals (explicit);
  - r± and p± by Krawczyk sweeps over real k-cells. C2-L's sweep code does this: 322 cells in 50 s for a COMPLEX square; real intervals are cheaper;
  - each loop checked nonsingular by box covers × k-cells (C2-L's tube code, about 40 s per loop and cell).
  - I estimate about an hour per spin for (0, k_cross).

### Q2. One homotopy class across the boundaries at k = 3 and k = 4, or different words?
- **k = 3 is not a construction boundary.** It is only the reach of route (i), which used the circle picture.
  - The circle |z| = R0 is fixed (R0 = 1.35 at 3/5), but the capsule lasso is redrawn per segment by the geometry rule.
  - So the continued F on (0, k_cross) is one function only if consecutive lassos are homotopic in the complement of the singular set, for every k in their overlap. That is certified by C2-L's homotopy swept-region check on real k-cells.
  - Wherever the rule switches homotopy class (if it ever does), the chain breaks into pieces. Each piece then needs its own nonzero point, one cheap point evaluation each.
- **k = 4 (the A/B switch) does change the words.**
  - Region B uses the Γ disc (winding 1 about r+, p+, p−) and a capsule lasso based at bB, against the circle (enclosing c0's branch cluster) and a capsule lasso based at R0.
  - These are different elements of π₁, so F_A and F_B are different analytic functions.
  - **The argument does not need them to agree.** The lemma needs, at each k, SOME pair of loops satisfying (i)–(iii). Region A sits inside the circle picture's domain (0, k_cross), and k_cross ≥ 4.02 > 4, so A never has to cross 4. Region B's certificates stand on their own words.
  - *To be certified, not assumed:* the circle picture's validity up to and past 4, i.e. the circle and the lassos nonsingular for real k up to a k_top > 4.0 (the closure check was numerical).

### Q3. What exactly would the theorem say?
**At spin 3/5, E = 4/5.** For every real k ∈ (0, 30/7] except at most finitely many k in D ⊂ [3, 4], the identity component of the differential Galois group of the NVE along the lower-axis orbit is SL₂(ℂ). Hence the geodesic flow has no additional first integral meromorphic in a neighbourhood of that orbit.
- It is **relative to the applicability chain** (Proposition 2, Lemma C, Lemma K), like Statement A.
- **D** is the set where F, tr M0, tr M1 or P_fin(tr M1), on the circle-picture words, vanishes. It is finite because each is real-analytic on (0, k_cross), not identically zero, and [3, 4] is compact inside (0, k_cross).
- **D avoids every certified stretch.** It cannot meet Statement A's interval, route (i)'s [1/640, 3], region B's certified segments, or the sliver.
- **A bound on #D (optional; cheap):** by Jensen's formula on a disc around [3, 4], N ≤ log(sup|F| / |F(k0)|) / log(R/r).
  - sup|F| on the bigger disc comes from Grönwall, ‖M‖ ≤ exp(∫‖A‖ |dz|) along the loops: crude, but rigorous and k-uniform for free once the loops are certified nonsingular there.
  - F(k0) is one rigorous point value. Even a sup of 1e50 gives an explicit N of the order of 100.
- **Numerically D = ∅.** The 50-digit argument-principle counts over (0, 4.05] at 3/5 find only the double zero at k = 0.
- **The same template covers the other gaps:**
  - the near-30/7 gap if ruling (B) fails, on region B's own words;
  - the small-k gaps at 3/10 and 9/10 (inside (0, k_cross), with Lemma K at 0);
  - 9/10's [2, 3].
  So the statement could be made at all four spins, each with its own finite exceptional set. **That is the high-value point:** it moves the claim from "numerical" to "a computer-assisted theorem except possibly finitely many k", without the region-A integration that failed.

## 3. What it would NOT do
- **It would not remove D.** The exceptions are finite, but not shown empty.
  - Removing them needs either a k-uniform certificate on [3, 4] (the route that failed), or a rigorous argument-principle count, which needs certified |F| bounds on a complex k-contour around [3, 4]. The Grönwall bound may be enough there, but it is untested.
- **It would not be stronger than the applicability chain,** and it would not reach energies other than E = 4/5 or other spins without their own loop-continuity certificates.
- **It says nothing physical beyond the existing claim.** The scope is unchanged: not black holes, off-axis regularity unchecked, null geodesics untested.
